\noindent\textbf{Key Observations:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High Fidelity at Low Rank:} Even with an aggressive projection rank $r=64$, SVD-Lin retains an average of $97.79\%$ of the baseline accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Parity at $r=128$:} Rank 128 achieves near parity with full attention, recovering $>98.8\%$ mean performance while substantially compressing the context state.
    \item \textbf{Asymptotic Convergence at $r=256$:} The performance gap completely vanishes at rank 256, falling within the stochastic standard deviation of the training loop ($\Delta \le 0.3\%$).
\end{itemize}

As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}, standard full softmax attention exhibits steep quadratic $O(N^2)$ growth in wall-clock time, whereas SVD-Lin maintains strict linear scaling with respect to sequence length.

\subsection{Ablation: Adaptive SVD vs.\ Random Orthonormal Projections}
To demonstrate the necessity of computing data-dependent singular vectors rather than static projections, we benchmark against a baseline that projects into a fixed random orthonormal subspace (as in Linformer). Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports performance on SST-2 and MNLI-m at rank $r=128$.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Ablation of projection methods at rank $r=128$.}
\label{tab:ablation}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Projection Scheme} & \textbf{SST-2 Acc (\%)} & \textbf{MNLI-m Acc (\%)} \\
\midrule
SVD-Lin (Data-Dependent SVD) & \textbf{92.2} & \textbf{83.1} \\
Random Fixed Projection     & 91.5          & 82.3          \\
Full Attention Baseline     & 93.0          & 84.3          \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent Data-dependent SVD projection consistently outperforms fixed random projection by $+0.7\%$ on SST-2 and $+0.8\%$ on MNLI, validating the theoretical premise that preserving principal singular energy subspaces yields superior context representation.

\subsection{Rank Sensitivity and Intrinsic Subspace Dimension}
We swept the truncation rank $r \in \{16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512\}$ to characterize the spectral decay properties of hidden activations. Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity} illustrates that accuracy increases sharply as $r$ expands from 16 to 64, subsequently plateauing beyond $r=128$. This highlights that natural language contextual manifolds exhibit low intrinsic dimensionality.
