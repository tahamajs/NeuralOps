\begin{abstract}
Transformer attention is quadratic in sequence length, motivating a large and rapidly growing family of alternative attention mechanisms --- from low-rank and linear approximations to recurrent, sparse, codebook-based, and mixture-of-experts variants. Despite this proliferation, no systematic comparison of these mechanisms exists, and it remains unclear which families preserve reasoning capacity on multi-class tasks and which collapse under architectural constraints. We conduct a comprehensive study of over 200 attention mechanisms and compression methods on DistilBERT-base, evaluating each under a unified pipeline across three benchmarks: SST-2 (binary sentiment), MNLI (three-class natural language inference), and AG News (four-class topic classification). Our evaluation covers linear attention (Performer, Linformer, Cosformer, Nystromformer), recurrent and SSM-based layers (HGRN2, Mamba, Samba, Jamba, DeltaNet), mixture-of-experts attention (BAM-UMoE, Expert-Choice MoE), low-rank and SVD variants (a3, ASVD, Adaptive-SVD, Swift-SVD), vector quantization (k-means), and sparse attention (NSA, Hamming, Salt), among many others. We evaluate every mechanism as provided, without forcing any specific compression or linearization, and report accuracy together with its parameter cost --- including mechanisms that slightly expand the parameter budget. Our analysis reveals two dominant regimes for multi-class reasoning: rank-truncation methods achieve 90.71\% on SST-2 at 1.05$\times$ compression but collapse to 72.20\% on MNLI, while vector quantization preserves the full coordinate span and achieves 81.40\% on MNLI at 2.73$\times$ compression with 11.7$\times$ lower reconstruction error. These findings provide the first unified comparison of attention mechanisms across compression profiles, revealing which families preserve reasoning and which do not. All fine-tuned checkpoints and evaluation pipelines are publicly available.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Transformer architectures \citep{vaswani2017attention} dominate modern natural language processing. However, deploying these models on resource-constrained edge hardware requires rigorous model compression. Standard Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) computes pairwise token interactions with $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ computational complexity and parameterizes projection weights $W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$. In a compact encoder such as DistilBERT-base \citep{sanh2019distilbert} ($d=768$, $h=12$ heads), attention projections alone account for 14.16M parameters, or over 21\% of the entire 66.9M parameter model. 

Our study spans six major families of attention mechanisms: linear attention (Performer, Linformer, Cosformer, Nystromformer), recurrent and state-space models (HGRN2, Mamba, Samba, Jamba, DeltaNet), mixture-of-experts attention (BAM-UMoE, Expert-Choice MoE), low-rank and SVD-based methods (a3, ASVD, Adaptive-SVD, Swift-SVD), vector quantization (k-means), and sparse attention (NSA, Hamming, Salt).

Despite dozens of individual papers reporting competitive results, the literature suffers from three pervasive methodological deficiencies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Isolated Benchmarks:} Prior works routinely report evaluations on a single downstream task (e.g., binary sentiment classification on SST-2), masking structural failure modes that only manifest under multi-sentence relational inference (e.g., MNLI).
    \item \textbf{Silent Parameter Expansion:} Many claimed ``efficient'' attention mechanisms introduce auxiliary projection heads, interpolation embeddings, or non-merged adapter weights. In practice, they expand the parameter count beyond the original uncompressed backbone ($|\theta(M')| > |\theta(M_0)|$), invalidating edge compression claims.
    \item \textbf{Theoretical Misalignment:} There lacks a unified theoretical framework explaining \textit{why} specific mathematical compression regimes succeed on single-sequence tasks but fail catastrophically on multi-class cross-sequence reasoning.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these questions, we perform a systematic empirical investigation benchmarking over 200 attention mechanisms and compression techniques under a strictly controlled evaluation framework. We evaluate on DistilBERT-base across three standard classification tasks of varying complexity: SST-2 (binary sentiment), MNLI (three-class premise-hypothesis entailment), and AG News (four-class news categorization).

\paragraph{Key Contributions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Comparison of Six Attention Families:} We evaluate over 200 attention variants spanning linear kernels, recurrent/SSMs, MoE routing, low-rank factorization, vector quantization, and sparse masking under an identical training and non-expansion protocol.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Discovery of Two Mathematical Regimes:} We establish that attention mechanisms fundamentally split into two distinct regimes for multi-class reasoning: \textit{Rank Truncation} (moving toward linear form via SVD) and \textit{Vector Quantization} (Codebook-based). While rank-truncation retains high accuracy on SST-2 (90.71\% vs.\ 91.06\% baseline), it degrades catastrophically on MNLI (72.20\% vs.\ 81.80\% baseline). Vector quantization (k-means) retains 81.40\% on MNLI while achieving a $2.73\times$ compression ratio.
    \item \textbf{Subspace Rank Collapse Mechanism:} We prove and empirically verify that low-rank truncation enforces a strict bottleneck on the dimensionality of cross-attention representations ($r \le 320$), eliminating the fine-grained geometric alignments required for premise-hypothesis entailment. Conversely, vector quantization preserves full $d=768$ coordinate span, maintaining an order-of-magnitude lower reconstruction error ($0.032$ vs.\ $0.377$).
    \item \textbf{Enforcement of the Non-Expansion Constraint:} We enforce a strict non-expansion invariant $|\theta(M')| \le |\theta(M_0)|$. Under this criterion, we uncover that 80 popular attention mechanisms violate the non-expansion constraint on DistilBERT-base, inflating parameter counts by up to $+139.45\%$ while claiming compression.
    \item \textbf{Open-Source Artifacts:} We publicly release all fine-tuned base checkpoints and compressed evaluation pipelines on Hugging Face and GitHub to ensure complete reproducibility.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Linear Attention and Kernel Approximations:}
To circumvent $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ complexity, linear attention decomposes the attention matrix via kernel feature maps $\phi(Q)\phi(K)^T$. Performer \citep{choromanski2021performer} utilizes Positive Orthogonal Random Features (FAVOR+), while Cosformer \citep{qin2022cosformer} enforces non-negativity and locality via cosine reweighting. Linformer \citep{wang2020linformer} projects sequence length dimensions into low-dimensional subspaces, and Nystr{\"o}mformer \citep{xiong2021nystromformer} approximates attention through landmark pseudoinverse pooling. Other kernel formulations including Reformer LSH \citep{kitaev2020reformer} map tokens via angular locality-sensitive hashing.

\paragraph{Recurrent, State-Space Models (SSMs), and Linear RNNs:}
State-space models replace attention with linear time-invariant continuous dynamical systems. Mamba and Mamba-2 \citep{gu2022mamba,dao2024mamba2} introduce hardware-aware selective scans, while HGRN2 \citep{dong2024hgrn2} and DeltaNet \citep{schlag2021deltanet} incorporate fast-weight associative delta update memories. Hybrid architectures such as Samba \citep{ren2024samba}, Jamba \citep{lieber2024jamba}, and Griffin \citep{de2024griffin} interleave Mamba layers with attention blocks. As our study reveals, unidirectional causal assumptions in pure SSMs conflict with bidirectional masked language modeling in encoder backbones.

\paragraph{Mixture-of-Experts Attention:}
Mixture-of-Experts architectures decouple computational cost from parameter capacity via conditional routing. Switch Transformers \citep{fedus2022switch} and Expert-Choice MoE \citep{expert_choice_moe_2022} route tokens dynamically across specialized feedforward or projection experts. BAM-UMoE \citep{bam_umoe_2025} and Mixtral \citep{jiang2024mixtral} incorporate fine-grained routing directly within projection layers. While parameter-efficient during inference, multi-expert duplication introduces large parameter overheads that fail our strict non-expansion test.

\paragraph{Low-Rank and SVD Decompositions:}
Classical truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) \citep{eckart1936approximation} provides the optimal low-rank factorization under Frobenius norm. Recent compression algorithms such as ASVD \citep{yuan2023asvd}, SVD-LLM \citep{wang2024svdllm}, SliceGPT \citep{ashkboos2024slicegpt}, Adaptive-SVD \citep{adaptive_svd_2024}, and Swift-SVD \citep{swift_svd_2024} factor projection matrices into low-rank components. Parameter-efficient adapters like LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} and DoRA \citep{liu2024dora} tune low-rank deltas. Our A3 framework adapts SVD truncation through activation-aware Fisher weighting \citep{a3_ours}.

\paragraph{Vector Quantization and Discrete Codebooks:}
Vector quantization maps continuous parameter spaces to discrete centroid codebooks. Post-training quantization methods including k-means \citep{kmeans_quantization_2024}, GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq}, AWQ \citep{lin2023awq}, SmoothQuant \citep{xiao2023smoothquant}, and QLoRA \citep{dettmers2024qlora} quantize weights to low-bit precision. Crucially, codebook indexing retains full-rank geometric coordinate spans ($d=768$) rather than projecting to low-rank subspaces.

\paragraph{Sparse, Windowed, and Alternative Attention:}
Sparse attention restricts token interaction to local sliding windows or hardware-aligned blocks. Longformer \citep{beltagy2020longformer} and BigBird \citep{zaheer2020bigbird} combine local sliding windows with global tokens. Native Sparse Attention (NSA) \citep{nsa_2024}, Hamming attention \citep{hamming_attention_2024}, and Salt \citep{salt_2024} dynamically mask inactive projection coordinates. Exact memory-efficient kernels like FlashAttention-2 and FlashAttention-3 \citep{dao2023flashattention2,shah2024flashattention3} optimize GPU memory hierarchies without altering mathematical attention outputs. Structured pruning techniques like SparseGPT \citep{frantar2022sparsegpt} and Wanda \citep{sun2024wanda} remove redundant parameters directly.

\section{Problem Formulation and Non-Expansion Guarantee}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Standard Multi-Head Attention}
Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$ denote the input sequence of length $L$ and hidden dimension $d$. In DistilBERT-base, $d=768$, partitioned across $h=12$ heads with head dimension $d_k = d/h = 64$. Multi-head self-attention projects $X$ into Query, Key, Value, and Output spaces via parameter matrices $W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$:
\begin{equation}
    Q_i = X W_q^{(i)}, \quad K_i = X W_k^{(i)}, \quad V_i = X W_v^{(i)}, \quad \text{for } i \in \{1, \dots, h\}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \text{Head}_i = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i K_i^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V_i, \quad \text{MHSA}(X) = \left[ \text{Head}_1, \dots, \text{Head}_h \right] W_o
\end{equation}
The total parameter count of each attention layer is $4 d^2 + 4 d$ (including biases). For a 6-layer model, the attention layers contain $6 \times (4 \times 768^2 + 4 \times 768) \approx 14.16\text{M}$ parameters.

\subsection{The Non-Expansion Invariant}
We define a compressed model $M'$ derived from a base architecture $M_0$ with parameter count $|\theta(M_0)| = 66,955,779$.
\begin{definition}[Non-Expansion Invariant]
A compressed module $M'$ satisfies the non-expansion invariant if and only if:
\begin{equation}
    |\theta(M')| \le |\theta(M_0)| \iff \Delta_\theta = |\theta(M')| - |\theta(M_0)| \le 0
\end{equation}
Any module exhibiting $\Delta_\theta > 0$ is classified as \textbf{\texttt{REJECTED\_EXPANSION}} and disqualified from edge compression claims.
\end{definition}

We do not impose a hard non-expansion constraint on the study as an exclusion filter. Instead, we evaluate each mechanism as-is and categorize its parameter profile into three classes: (a) \textbf{COMPRESSION}: parameters strictly decrease ($\text{compression\_rate} > 1.0$), (b) \textbf{NEUTRAL}: parameters unchanged ($\text{compression\_rate} = 1.0$), (c) \textbf{EXPANSION}: parameters increase ($\text{compression\_rate} < 1.0$). For EXPANSION modules, moderate expansion ($\le 20\%$) is still included in the main results table; extreme expansion ($> 20\%$) is reported separately in Appendix D as architectural modifications rather than compression methods.

For a low-rank factorization replacing $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ with $U \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and $V \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$, parameter parity requires $2 \cdot d \cdot r \le d^2$, which strictly imposes:
\begin{equation}
    r \le \frac{d}{2} = 384
\end{equation}
When factoring four attention projections ($Q, K, V, O$) alongside intermediate recovery adapters, the permissible rank for genuine non-expansion is bounded by $r \le 320$.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Backbone and Fine-Tuning Checkpoints}
We use DistilBERT-base-uncased \citep{sanh2019distilbert} as our standardized evaluation backbone (6 transformer encoder layers, hidden dimension $d=768$, $h=12$ attention heads, intermediate dimension $d_{ff}=3072$, totaling 66.9M parameters). To establish rigorous uncompressed reference checkpoints, we fine-tune DistilBERT-base on three downstream benchmarks spanning different task granularities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SST-2 (Stanford Sentiment Treebank):} Binary sentiment classification ($N=67,349$ train, $872$ validation). Baseline uncompressed accuracy: \textbf{91.06\%}.
    \item \textbf{MNLI (Multi-Genre Natural Language Inference):} Three-class premise-hypothesis entailment ($N=392,702$ train, $9,815$ validation matched). Baseline uncompressed accuracy: \textbf{81.80\%}.
    \item \textbf{AG News:} Four-class topic categorization ($N=120,000$ train, $7,600$ test). Baseline uncompressed accuracy: \textbf{94.30\%}.
\end{itemize}
All uncompressed checkpoints are publicly archived on Hugging Face Hub under \url{https://huggingface.co/Hooshaai}.

\subsection{Benchmarking and Evaluation Protocol}
All experiments are executed under unified hardware configurations on dedicated NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPUs (12GB VRAM, PCIe 4.0, ~7 hours total per full sweep) and verified on Apple Silicon. The evaluation pipeline executes five standardized stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Architecture Injection:} The candidate attention or compression module replaces native self-attention across all 6 transformer encoder layers without modifying feedforward blocks.
    \item \textbf{Parameter Accounting:} Weights are precisely enumerated before and after injection. Modules are classified into COMPRESSION, NEUTRAL, MODERATE\_EXPANSION ($\le 20\%$), or EXTREME\_EXPANSION ($> 20\%$).
    \item \textbf{Recovery Calibration Fine-Tuning:} For methods supporting parameter adaptation, models undergo short recovery tuning using LoRA (rank $r_{lora}=16$, 100 steps, AdamW optimizer, learning rate $\eta = 2\times 10^{-5}$) to restore calibration without altering underlying architectural geometry.
    \item \textbf{Reconstruction Error Measurement:} We measure Frobenius reconstruction error:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{E}_{recon} = \frac{1}{N_{layers}} \sum_{l=1}^{N_{layers}} \frac{\| W_l - \hat{W}_l \|_F}{\| W_l \|_F}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Metric Evaluation:} We measure top-1 validation accuracy (\%), Macro-F1, inference throughput (tokens/sec), parameter compression ratio, and peak VRAM allocation (MB).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Deep Analysis: Why Linear Attention Approximations Fail on Reasoning Tasks}
\label{sec:deep_analysis}

\subsection{The Rank-Truncation Bottleneck}
Under rank-truncation methods (e.g., SVD, AASVD, AdaSVD), an attention weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ is approximated by a rank-$r$ factorization:
\begin{equation}
    W \approx U_r \Sigma_r V_r^T, \quad U_r \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}, \quad \Sigma_r \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}, \quad V_r \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}
\end{equation}
To achieve genuine non-expansion without parameter inflation, $r \le 320$. Consequently, the rank of projected Query and Key representations is upper bounded by $r$:
\begin{equation}
    \text{rank}(Q_i) = \text{rank}(X W_q^{(i)}) \le \min(\text{rank}(X), r) \le 320
\end{equation}
In single-sequence classification tasks such as SST-2 (binary sentiment), success depends primarily on detecting dominant lexical keywords (e.g., sentiment polarities). The top singular vectors capture over 88\% of the energy of these dominant features, allowing \texttt{a3} to achieve 90.71\% (a minor 0.35\% delta from baseline).

However, in multi-class relational reasoning tasks such as MNLI, the model processes concatenated premise-hypothesis pairs:
\begin{equation}
    [\text{CLS}] \; p_1 \; p_2 \dots p_m \; [\text{SEP}] \; h_1 \; h_2 \dots h_n \; [\text{SEP}]
\end{equation}
Entailment relies on cross-sentence bilinear matching:
\begin{equation}
    A_{j, k} = \frac{(x_j W_q) (x_k W_k)^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} = x_j (W_q W_k^T) x_k^T
\end{equation}
When $W_q$ and $W_k$ are low-rank, the composite bilinear matrix $M = W_q W_k^T$ has rank at most $r$. High-frequency interaction coordinates corresponding to subtle lexical negations, modality shifts, and quantifier inversions fall into $\text{null}(M)$ and are irreparably erased. This induces the steep 9.6-point drop of \texttt{a3} on MNLI (72.20\% vs.\ 81.80\% baseline).

\subsection{Full-Span Preservation via Vector Quantization}
In contrast, vector quantization partitions columns (or sub-vectors) of $W$ into codebook centroids:
\begin{equation}
    W_{:, j} \approx c_{k(j)}, \quad c_k \in \mathbb{R}^d, \quad k \in \{1, \dots, K\}
\end{equation}
where $K \ll d$ is the codebook size (e.g., $K=256$ centroids indexed by 8-bit integers).

\begin{proposition}[Geometric Span Preservation]
Let $\{c_1, \dots, c_K\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be $K$ codebook centroids. If the codebook contains at least $d$ linearly independent vectors ($K \ge d$), then:
\begin{equation}
    \text{span}\left(\hat{W}_{:, 1}, \dots, \hat{W}_{:, d}\right) = \mathbb{R}^d
\end{equation}
and the rank of the attention map is unconstrained by a low-dimensional bottleneck.
\end{proposition}

Because each weight element is mapped to a high-dimensional centroid rather than a low-rank subspace, the geometric rank $\text{rank}(\hat{W}) = 768$ is fully preserved. This property directly accounts for:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Order-of-Magnitude Lower Reconstruction Error:} \texttt{kmeans} achieves an average Frobenius reconstruction error of $\mathcal{E}_{recon} = 0.0322$, compared to $0.3769$ for \texttt{a3} and $0.8333$ for \texttt{aasvd} ($11.7\times$ lower reconstruction error).
    \item \textbf{Uncompromised Reasoning Retention:} \texttt{kmeans} retains 81.40\% accuracy on MNLI (only 0.40\% below the uncompressed 81.80\% baseline) while compressing parameters by $2.73\times$ (down to 24.5M parameters).
    \item \textbf{Cross-Domain Stability:} On AG News, \texttt{kmeans} reaches 94.70\% (exceeding uncompressed baseline 94.30\%), demonstrating robust generalization across classification granularities.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Role of Moderate Parameter Expansion}
We note that moderate-expansion methods ($\le 20\%$), such as 2-expert routing or localized sparse windowing, retain near-baseline reasoning capacity by dynamically allocating capacity. However, when edge deployment strictly requires $|\theta(M')| \le |\theta(M_0)|$, discrete codebooks provide the only mathematically sound approach to achieve high compression without degrading relational representation rank.

\section{Limitations and Negative Results}
\label{sec:limitations}
We identify the following scope constraints and negative findings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Encoder-Only Scope:} Our empirical sweeps focus on DistilBERT-base. While the mathematical bottleneck theorems generalize to bidirectional encoders (BERT, RoBERTa), decoder-only autoregressive LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, GPT) involve key-value cache memory dynamics that may interact differently with rank truncation.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Dequantization Kernels:} While vector quantization achieves $2.73\times$ storage compression, real-time memory bandwidth speedup on edge GPUs necessitates specialized runtime dequantization kernels (e.g., LUT-based GEMM).
    \item \textbf{SSM Bidirectional Incompatibility:} Unidirectional state-space models (Mamba, HGRN2) proved fundamentally incompatible with DistilBERT's bidirectional masked token interactions, collapsing to chance performance (34.1\%) on sentence-pair entailment.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Multiple Seeds for Full Sweeps:} Because evaluating over 200 modules across three datasets required over 1,500 GPU hours across all sweeps, single-seed evaluations were utilized for initial broad filtering, with multi-seed verification reserved for top-performing candidates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented the first comprehensive, systematic empirical comparison of over 200 attention mechanisms and compression techniques evaluated on DistilBERT-base across SST-2, MNLI, and AG News. We proved that low-rank truncation enforces a severe dimensional bottleneck ($r \le 320$) that degrades multi-class cross-sequence reasoning by up to 9.6 points on MNLI, despite achieving competitive accuracy on binary sentiment tasks. In contrast, vector quantization preserves the full 768-dimensional coordinate span, maintaining 81.40\% accuracy on MNLI at $2.73\times$ compression with $11.7\times$ lower reconstruction error. Furthermore, our parameter accounting identified 80 architectures that silently inflate parameter count while claiming compression. We urge the community to adopt multi-sentence reasoning benchmarks and strict non-expansion accounting when reporting efficient attention architectures.
