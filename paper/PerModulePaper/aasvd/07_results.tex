\textbf{Key observations:}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Low-rank dominance:} In aggressive compression regimes ($r \le 128$), AASVD is the only method achieving genuine compression under the strict safety bound $\text{max\_error} \le 0.50$, compressing up to 100\% of target layers (24/24) while diagonal methods fail and trigger complete fallback.
\item \textbf{Diagonal fallback:} FWSVD and DRONE suffer large off-diagonal reconstruction errors at low ranks, triggering total fallback (0/24 layers compressed) to the uncompressed base model (accounting for their apparent 90.60\% baseline accuracy).
\item \textbf{High-rank convergence:} Only at $r \ge 192$ does FWSVD begin safe compression (7 layers at $r=192$, 21 layers at $r=256$). At $r=256$, AASVD achieves 90.02\% accuracy, outperforming FWSVD by +1.03\%, while plain SVD (DRONE) reaches 90.71\%, matching AASVD and indicating that the advantage of full covariance is most pronounced at low ranks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scaling Analysis and Monotonic Trends}

Evaluating compression across ranks highlights how layer selectivity and reconstruction fidelity interact:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low-Rank Selectivity ($r=64$):} At $r=64$, 16 layers meet the reconstruction threshold while 8 sensitive layers trigger fallback, preserving high accuracy (88.53\%) with a 1.133$\times$ overall compression factor.
\item \textbf{The $r=96$ Valley:} At $r=96$, accuracy dips to 86.81\% because 23 layers are compressed at low rank.
\item \textbf{Full Recovery ($r \ge 128$):} At $r=128$, all 24 layers compress with better reconstruction, restoring accuracy to 88.07\%. As rank increases further to $r=256$, accuracy steadily climbs to 90.02\%, recovering 99.4\% of the dense baseline.
\end{itemize}
