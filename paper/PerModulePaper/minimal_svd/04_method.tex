
\section{Method}

\subsection{Overview}

MinimalSVD replaces the four attention projections of a transformer layer 
with \textbf{SVD-factorized linear modules}:

\[
W \to (U, V), \qquad
y = (x V^\top) U^\top + \text{LoRA}(x)
\tag{4.1}
\]

The factorization is done independently per projection. Rank is chosen 
adaptively per layer based on spectral energy.

\textbf{Figure 4.1} shows the pipeline.

\begin{lstlisting}
   +----------------------------------------------+
   |  Input weight W $\in$ R^{d_out $\times$ d_in}           |
   +--------------------+-------------------------+
                        |
                         
   +----------------------------------------------+
   |  SVD: W = U $\Sigma$ V^T                             |
   |  Compute cumulative energy E(r)              |
   |  r_eff = min(r_max, argmin E(r) $\ge$  )         |
   +--------------------+-------------------------+
                        |
                         
   +----------------------------------------------+
   |  Truncate and symmetrize                      |
   |  U_r = U[:, :r]   $\sqrt{}$$\Sigma$[:r]                     |
   |  V_r = $\sqrt{}$$\Sigma$[:r]   V[:r, :]^T                   |
   +--------------------+-------------------------+
                        |
                         
   +----------------------------------------------+
   |  Store in FactorisedLinear                    |
   |  Freeze U, V; add trainable LoRA              |
   +----------------------------------------------+
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The `FactorisedLinear` Module}

\subsubsection{Forward Pass}

The module computes:

\[
y = \text{up}(\text{down}(x)) + \text{LoRA}(x)
\tag{4.2}
\]

where:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{down: nn.Linear(d\_in, r, bias=False)} -- stores $V_r \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_\text{in}}$
\item \texttt{up: nn.Linear(r, d\_out, bias=True)} -- stores $U_r \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\text{out} \times r}$
\item \texttt{LoRA(x) = (x A^\top) B^\top \cdot s} -- optional adapter

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Spectral Energy-Adaptive Rank Selection}

\subsubsection{Algorithm}

Given weight $W$ and hyperparameters $r_\text{max}$ (cap) and $\eta$ (energy):

\begin{enumerate}
\item Compute SVD: $W = U \Sigma V^\top$.
\item Compute cumulative energy:
  $$
  E(r) = \frac{\sum\_{i=1}^{r} \sigma\_i^2}{\sum\_i \sigma\_i^2}
  \tag{4.3}
  $$
\item Find the smallest $r$ with $E(r) \geq \eta$:
  $$
  r\_\text{energy} = \left|\{i : E(i) < \eta\}\right| + 1
  \tag{4.4}
  $$
\item Cap the rank:
  $$
  r\_\text{eff} = \min(r\_\text{max}, r\_\text{energy}, \min(d\_\text{in}, d\_\text{out}))
  \tag{4.5}
  $$

\end{enumerate}
\subsubsection{Why Energy?}

The energy criterion captures \textbf{how concentrated the spectrum is}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rank-1 matrix} ($\sigma_{i>1} = 0$): $r_\text{energy} = 1$ for any
  $\eta > 0$. Very compressible.
\item \textbf{Identity matrix} ($\sigma_i = 1$): $r_\text{energy} = \lceil \eta d \rceil$.
  Less compressible.

\end{itemize}
Real transformer weights lie between these extremes, with power-law or 
exponential decay.

\subsubsection{Comparison to Fixed Rank}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Criterion} & \textbf{Pros} & \textbf{Cons} \\
\midrule
Fixed rank & Simple & Ignores heterogeneity \\
Energy & Adaptive & No task-specific calibration \\
Fisher-weighted & Task-aware & Requires calibration \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

MinimalSVD uses energy: \textbf{training-free} and applicable wherever the model 
weights are available.

\subsection{Weight Factorization}

\subsubsection{Symmetric Distribution}

We distribute $\Sigma^{1/2}$ between the two factors:

\[
U_r = U_{:, :r} \Sigma_{:r, :r}^{1/2}, \qquad
V_r = \Sigma_{:r, :r}^{1/2} V_{:r, :}^\top
\tag{4.6}
\]

This gives $W_r = U_r V_r$ with balanced magnitudes, improving training 
stability.

\subsubsection{Storage}

The framework stores $V_r$ in \texttt{down.weight} and $U_r$ in \texttt{up.weight}:

\begin{lstlisting}
self.down.weight.data[:r, :] = V_r    # (r, d_in)
self.up.weight.data[:, :r] = U_r      # (d_out, r)
\end{lstlisting}

For $r < \text{target rank}$, remaining rows/columns are zero.

\subsection{Weight Transfer from Pretrained Attention}

MinimalSVD extracts $W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O$ from the pretrained attention. This 
is nontrivial because different architectures store them differently.

\subsubsection{Extraction Path (per architecture)}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
\textbf{Architecture} & \textbf{$W_Q$} & \textbf{$W_K$} & \textbf{$W_V$} & \textbf{$W_O$} \\
\midrule
DistilBERT & \texttt{q\_lin} & \texttt{k\_lin} & \texttt{v\_lin} & \texttt{out\_lin} \\
RoBERTa & \texttt{self.query} & \texttt{self.key} & \texttt{self.value} & \texttt{output.dense} \\
GPT-2 & \texttt{c\_attn[:d]} & \texttt{c\_attn[d:2d]} & \texttt{c\_attn[2d:]} & \texttt{c\_proj} \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsubsection{GPT-2 Fused c\_attn}

GPT-2 fuses $Q, K, V$ into one \texttt{c\_attn}:

\[
W_{c\_attn} = \begin{pmatrix} W_Q \\ W_K \\ W_V \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{3d \times d}
\tag{4.7}
\]

(Conv1D stores this transposed.) MinimalSVD splits:

\begin{lstlisting}
w = src.c_attn.weight.data.clone()
if is_conv1d:
    w = w.t()   # (3d, d)
q_w, k_w, v_w = w.chunk(3, dim=0)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{RoBERTa Nested}

The output projection $W_O$ is at \texttt{attention.output.dense}, \textbf{outside} the 
inner attention. When replacing the inner attention, the outer \texttt{output.dense} 
remains in the model and applies the output projection \textbf{after} our module 
returns. So we set \texttt{is\_nested\_encoder = True} and skip \texttt{out\_proj} in forward.

\subsubsection{GQA (Qwen)}

For GQA, $H_{kv} < H$. MinimalSVD factorizes each at its natural dimension 
and applies \texttt{repeat\_kv} at forward.

\subsection{Validation}

\subsubsection{Frobenius Norm Validation}

Extracted weights are validated:

\begin{lstlisting}
for proj in ["q", "k", "v"]:
    w = extracted[proj][0]
    if w is not None:
        norm = torch.linalg.norm(w.float())
        if norm < 1e-4:
            raise RuntimeError(f"Near-zero weight on {proj}")
\end{lstlisting}

This catches catastrophic extraction failures.

\subsubsection{Missing Weight Check}

\begin{lstlisting}
missing = [k for k in ["q", "k", "v"] if extracted[k][0] is None]
if not self.is_nested_encoder and extracted["o"][0] is None:
    missing.append("o")
if missing:
    raise RuntimeError(f"Missing required weights: {missing}")
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{LoRA Recovery}

After factorization, base weights are frozen:

\begin{lstlisting}
def freeze_base_weights(self):
    self.down.weight.requires_grad = False
    self.up.weight.requires_grad = False
    if self.up.bias is not None:
        self.up.bias.requires_grad = False
    if self.lora_rank > 0:
        self.lora_A.requires_grad = True
        self.lora_B.requires_grad = True
\end{lstlisting}

Only \texttt{lora\_A} and \texttt{lora\_B} are trained. LoRA $B$ starts at zero, so:

\[
y_{\text{init}} = (x V^\top) U^\top + b
\tag{4.8}
\]

which equals the SVD approximation.

\subsection{The Full Algorithm}

\textbf{Algorithm 1:} MinimalSVD injection (single-layer)
\begin{lstlisting}
Input:  layer  , config c, energy  , rank cap r_max
Output: layer with factorized attention

1:  src $\leftarrow$ get_attention(layer)
2:  if src is already MinimalSVD: return None
3:
4:  new_attn $\leftarrow$ MinimalSVDAttention(c)
5:  try:
6:      new_attn.copy_weights(src)
7:  except Exception:
8:      return None
9:
10: # Set nested flag
11: if hasattr(layer, "attention") and hasattr(layer.attention, "self"):
12:     new_attn.is_nested_encoder = True
13:     layer.attention.self = new_attn
14: else:
15:     new_attn.is_nested_encoder = False
16:     if hasattr(layer, "attention"):
17:         layer.attention = new_attn
18:     elif hasattr(layer, "self"):
19:         new_attn.is_nested_encoder = True
20:         layer.self = new_attn
21: return new_attn
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Whole-model traversal} is delegated to the caller:

\begin{lstlisting}
for layer in model.transformer.layer:
    inject_minimal_svd(layer, model.config, energy=0.95)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Theoretical Notes}

\subsubsection{Optimality per Rank}

Given rank $r$, the truncated SVD is optimal in Frobenius norm by 
Eckart--Young.

\subsubsection{Energy Error Bound}

\textbf{Proposition.} If $r_\text{eff}$ is the smallest rank such that 
$E(r_\text{eff}) \geq \eta$, then:

\[
\frac{\|W - W_{r_\text{eff}}\|_F}{\|W\|_F} \leq \sqrt{1 - \eta}
\tag{4.9}
\]

\textbf{Proof.} $\|W - W_r\|_F^2 = \|W\|_F^2(1 - E(r))$. Since 
$E(r_\text{eff}) \geq \eta$, the bound follows. $\square$

\textbf{Corollary.} For $\eta = 0.95$: relative error $\leq \sqrt{0.05} \approx 0.224$. 
For $\eta = 0.99$: relative error $\leq 0.10$.

\subsubsection{Symmetric Distribution Stability}

Distributing $\Sigma^{1/2}$ symmetrically ensures both factors have similar 
spectral norms, improving training stability in FP16.

\subsection{Summary}

MinimalSVD:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Factorizes each attention projection $W \to (U, V)$.
\item Selects rank via spectral energy $\eta$.
\item Uses LoRA for recovery.
\item Handles four attention architectures.
\item Achieves \textbf{real parameter reduction} of $d^2 / (r(d_\text{in} + d_\text{out}))$.
\end{enumerate}
