\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Why Does Energy Thresholding Work?}

\textbf{Figure 8.1} shows the singular value spectrum of the four projections in 
a middle DistilBERT layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Q:} rapid decay (top 128 capture {{D\_Q\_ENERGY128}}\%)
\item \textbf{K:} rapid decay (top 128 capture {{D\_K\_ENERGY128}}\%)
\item \textbf{V:} moderate decay (top 128 capture {{D\_V\_ENERGY128}}\%)
\item \textbf{O:} slower decay (top 128 capture {{D\_O\_ENERGY128}}\%)

\end{itemize}
The adaptive threshold allocates:
\begin{itemize}
\item Small rank to Q, K (rapid decay)
\item Larger rank to V, O (moderate/slow decay)

\end{itemize}
\textbf{Fixed-rank methods over- or under-compress each projection.}

\subsection{T5 Relative Bias Preservation}

We verify that the relative attention bias is preserved:

\begin{lstlisting}
assert self.relative_attention_bias is not None
assert self.relative_attention_bias.shape == src.relative_attention_bias.shape
assert torch.allclose(self.relative_attention_bias, src.relative_attention_bias)
\end{lstlisting}

The bias is added to attention scores in the forward pass, providing 
position information on top of the factorized Q/K/V.

\subsubsection{RoBERTa Stability}

RoBERTa's loss curve is less smooth than DistilBERT's, likely due to the 
nested structure and 12 layers of compounding error.

\subsection{Summary of Insights}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Energy thresholding is principled} -- hard bound on reconstruction.
\item \textbf{Adaptive rank beats fixed rank} at equal compression.
\item \textbf{V, O need larger rank} than Q, K.
\item \textbf{$\tau$ = 0.92 is a sweet spot.}
\item \textbf{T5 relative bias preserved correctly.}

\end{enumerate}
