 A.1 Equivalence of Covariance-Weighted SVD
We minimize $\|(W - \hat{W}) X^	op\|\_F^2$. Let $C\_X = 
rac{1}{N} X^	op X = C\_X^{1/2} C\_X^{1/2}$.
Then $\|(W - \hat{W}) X^	op\|\_F^2 = N \|(W - \hat{W}) C\_X^{1/2}\|\_F^2$.
Letting $	ilde{W} = W C\_X^{1/2}$ and $	ilde{W}\_r = \hat{W} C\_X^{1/2}$, the Eckart - Young theorem guarantees that the optimal rank-$r$ approximation $	ilde{W}\_r$ is obtained via standard truncated SVD of $	ilde{W}$. Multiplying by $C\_X^{-1/2}$ on the right yields the optimal unweighted factor $V\_r$.


 1. Executive Abstract \& Theoretical Motivation


Standard SVD acts purely on static weight matrices $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d\_{\text{out}} \times d\_{\text{in}}}$, operating under the implicit assumption that all input directions in $\mathbb{R}^{d\_{\text{in}}}$ are equally probable. However, in transformer architectures, input activations $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N \times d\_{\text{in}}}$ lie on low-dimensional non-linear manifolds due to layer normalization, residual connections, and token correlation. Truncating weight singular vectors that align strongly with high-variance activation subspaces causes catastrophic perplexity spikes.

`AASVDCompressor` (Anchored Adaptive SVD) resolves this by anchoring low-rank SVD decomposition directly to the empirical activation subspace. By capturing input activation tensors during forward calibration passes, the engine computes the right-singular vectors of the activations via SVD to form an anchor projection matrix $P\_{\text{anchor}}$. The weight matrix $W$ is projected onto this activation anchor space prior to low-rank factorization, ensuring that singular component truncation preserves the activation directions essential to network function.
