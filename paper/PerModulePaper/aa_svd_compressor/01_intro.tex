\section{01 Intro}

 1. Introduction

 1.1 Motivation
In standard transformer architectures such as DistilBERT, RoBERTa, and GPT-2, linear projection layers ($W\_Q, W\_K, W\_V, W\_O$ in multi-head attention and intermediate/output layers in feed-forward networks) dominate total parameter storage and runtime computational budgets. For instance, in DistilBERT ($d=768$), each self-attention projection accounts for $768 \times 768 = 589,824$ parameters. Physical low-rank matrix factorization replaces $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d\_{\text{out}} \times d\_{\text{in}}}$ with factor matrices $U \in \mathbb{R}^{d\_{\text{out}} \times r}$ and $V \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d\_{\text{in}}}$, reducing parameter complexity from $d\_{\text{in}} d\_{\text{out}}$ down to $r(d\_{\text{in}} + d\_{\text{out}})$.

However, naive truncated SVD minimizes unweighted Frobenius reconstruction error $\|W - \hat{W}\|\_F^2$, implicitly assuming that all feature dimensions in the input space are independently and identically distributed. In practice, modern deep language models develop pronounced outlier channels with activation variances up to $100\times$ larger than typical channels. Truncating singular components without accounting for activation magnitude results in severe representation collapse.

 1.2 Our Approach
To resolve this limitation, `aa\_svd\_compressor` computes empirical input activation covariances over multi-domain calibration text tokens. By preconditioning weights prior to singular value decomposition and unweighting singular vectors during layer construction, the factorization minimizes activation-weighted reconstruction error $\|(W - \hat{W}) X\|\_F^2$. The compressed model replaces target dense layers with structural `SVDLinear` modules that execute two sequential lower-dimensional matrix multiplications, achieving true physical parameter reduction and FLOP savings without requiring custom sparse kernels.
