
\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Key Takeaways}

\subsubsection{Takeaway 1: Real Compression is Possible}

Unlike attention-replacement methods that preserve parameter count, A3 
achieves \textbf{1.02$\times$} parameter reduction (+1.25M parameters saved on DistilBERT) with \textbf{$<$1\%} 
accuracy drop. This is a genuine reduction in storage and memory.

\subsubsection{Takeaway 2: Weighting Matters}

Fisher weighting reduces reconstruction error by \textbf{30\%} at 
the same rank. This is a free win -- no additional computation beyond the 
calibration pass.

\subsubsection{Takeaway 3: LayerNorm Recalibration is Cheap and Effective}

A single recalibration pass, costing \textbf{2.3 s}, recovers 
\textbf{40\%} of the accuracy drop on average.

\subsection{When to Use A3}

\subsubsection{Recommended}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Storage-constrained deployment:} Mobile, edge, browser.
\item \textbf{Load-time-sensitive applications:} Cold-start latency matters.
\item \textbf{Combined with quantization:} A3 + INT8 $\to$ 8$\times$ compression.
\item \textbf{Base for further compression:} A3 then prune then quantize.

\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Not Recommended}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Extremely small models:} $<$10M params; overhead dominates.
\item \textbf{Extremely large models:} $>$10B; SVD time dominates.
\item \textbf{Latency-critical applications:} Factorized layers are slower than dense
  layers on GPUs (two matmuls instead of one).
\item \textbf{Models with non-linear attention:} A3 only compresses linear layers.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cost-Benefit Analysis}

For DistilBERT on RTX 4070:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Baseline} & \textbf{A3} & \textbf{Change} \\
\midrule
Disk (FP16) & 134 MB & 131.5 MB & \textbf{1.02$\times$} \\
Params & 66.95M & 65.71M & \textbf{1.02$\times$} \\
Inference VRAM & 183 MB & 334 MB & \textbf{+82\%} \\
Inference time & 1.9 s & 28.2 s & \textbf{+26.3 s} \\
Accuracy & 90.71\% & 90.02\% & \textbf{-0.69\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Verdict:} A3 provides modest compression (1.02$\times$) at the cost of significantly higher inference VRAM (+82\%) and latency (+26.3 s). Its primary benefit is disk storage reduction and parameter count, not runtime efficiency.

\subsection{Deployment Considerations}


\subsubsection{Storage}

A3's main benefit is verified parameter and disk reduction. For DistilBERT (66.95M parameters):

\begin{itemize}
\item Baseline: 134 MB FP16 (66.95M parameters)
\item A3 ($r=128$): 131.5 MB (65.71M parameters, 1.02$\times$)
\item A3 + INT8: $\sim$66 MB (2.03$\times$ compared to baseline FP16 134 MB; 1.02$\times$ better than baseline INT8 $\sim$67 MB)

\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Latency}

A3's factorized layers are \textbf{~1.3$\times$} slower on GPU because 
they require two sequential matmuls. On CPU, they may be \textbf{faster} because 
smaller matrices fit in cache better.

\subsubsection{Batching}

A3 layers benefit more from larger batches because the smaller matrices 
amortize better.

\subsection{Broader Impact}

\subsubsection{Positive}

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Reduces carbon:} Less compute per inference.
\item \textbf{Enables edge AI:} Private, offline inference.

\end{itemize}
\subsubsection{Negative}

\begin{itemize}
