\section{Related Work and Theoretical Foundations}

\subsection{Model Compression Paradigms}
Transformer compression literature focuses on parameter and memory reduction through distinct mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Low-Rank Decomposition:} Decomposing high-dimensional weight matrices into rank-$r$ bottleneck factors via truncated SVD, randomized linear algebra, or Tucker/Tensor-Train decompositions~\citep{eckart1936,halko2011,wang2024svdllm,yuan2023asvd,hsu2022fwsvd,chen2021drone,lan2020albert,novikov2015tensor}. Low-rank methods exploit the empirical observation that overparameterized weight matrices possess rapidly decaying singular spectra.
\item \textbf{Quantization and Discretization:} Mapping continuous parameters into low-bit integers (INT8, INT4) or vector-quantized codebooks~\citep{frantar2023gptq,lin2024awq,xiao2023smoothquant}. While effective, extreme quantization requires careful calibration to prevent activation outlier degradation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sensitivity-Aware Saliency and Activation Whitening}
Weight magnitude alone often fails to correlate with functional layer saliency. Modern compression systems leverage first-order Taylor expansions, empirical Fisher information matrices, and activation covariance statistics to preserve salient subspace representations under aggressive parameter reduction~\citep{kirkpatrick2017,molchanov2019}:
\[
S_{ij} = \left| \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_{ij}} W_{ij} \right| \approx |W_{ij}| \cdot \mathbb{E}[|X_j|]
\]
By rotating weights into the empirical activation covariance principal basis, activation-aware methods minimize output reconstruction distortion.

\subsection{Efficient Attention Formulations}
Standard softmax attention requires computing an $N \times N$ dense matrix~\citep{vaswani2017}:
\[
\text{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V
\]
Kernelized linear attention and low-rank approximation reformulate this via positive feature maps or matrix factorization~\citep{katharopoulos2020,choromanski2021,xiong2021nystromformer}, enabling associative evaluation:
\[
(Q K^\top) V = Q (K^\top V)
\]
This shifts computational complexity from quadratic $\mathcal{O}(N^2 d)$ to linear $\mathcal{O}(N d^2)$. The \texttt{AdaptiveRanks} compressor leverages these structural properties to optimize inference speed and VRAM overhead.
