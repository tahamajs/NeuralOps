\title{Toward Linear Attention: A Comprehensive Comparison of Over 200 Attention Mechanisms}

\author{Anonymous authors}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Transformer attention is quadratic in sequence length, motivating a large and rapidly growing family of alternative attention mechanisms --- from low-rank and linear approximations to recurrent, sparse, codebook-based, and mixture-of-experts variants. Despite this proliferation, no systematic comparison of these mechanisms exists, and it remains unclear which families preserve reasoning capacity on multi-class tasks and which collapse under architectural constraints. We conduct a comprehensive study of over 200 attention mechanisms and compression methods on DistilBERT-base, evaluating each under a unified pipeline across three benchmarks: SST-2 (binary sentiment), MNLI (three-class natural language inference), and AG News (four-class topic classification). Our evaluation covers linear attention (Performer, Linformer, Cosformer, Nystromformer), recurrent and SSM-based layers (HGRN2, Mamba, Samba, Jamba, DeltaNet), mixture-of-experts attention (BAM-UMoE, Expert-Choice MoE), low-rank and SVD variants (a3, ASVD, Adaptive-SVD, Swift-SVD), vector quantization (k-means), and sparse attention (NSA, Hamming, Salt), among many others. Under a strict non-expansion guarantee ($|\theta(M')| \le |\theta(M_0)|$), we find that these mechanisms fall into two regimes for multi-class reasoning: rank-truncation methods move attention toward a linear form and achieve 90.71\% on SST-2 at 1.05$\times$ compression but collapse to 72.20\% on MNLI, while vector quantization preserves the full coordinate span and achieves 81.40\% on MNLI at 2.73$\times$ compression with 11.7$\times$ lower reconstruction error. These findings provide the first unified comparison of attention mechanisms toward linear attention, revealing which families preserve reasoning and which do not. All fine-tuned checkpoints and evaluation pipelines are publicly available.
\end{abstract}
