TITLE:
Rank Truncation vs. Vector Quantization: A Systematic Study of Attention Compression for Multi-Class Reasoning

ABSTRACT:
Attention compression reduces the memory and compute footprint of transformer models, yet the comparative efficacy of divergent compression paradigms remains poorly understood. We present a systematic empirical study of over 200 attention variants and compression methods on DistilBERT-base across three benchmarks: SST-2 (binary sentiment), MNLI (three-class natural language inference), and AG News (four-class topic classification). Our central finding reveals that attention compression methods operate within two distinct mathematical regimes. Rank-truncation methods (SVD-based) constrain projection matrices to low-rank subspaces (r <= 320 for non-expansion); while achieving 90.71% accuracy on SST-2 at 1.05x compression, they degrade sharply to 72.30% on MNLI (a 9.5-point drop from the 81.80% baseline). Conversely, vector quantization methods preserve the full 768-dimensional span while quantizing weights into centroid codebooks; our k-means implementation achieves 90.37% on SST-2 and 81.40% on MNLI at 2.73x compression, yielding a 9.10-point advantage over SVD alongside an order-of-magnitude lower reconstruction error (0.032 vs. 0.377). We demonstrate that low-rank truncation collapses the cross-sentence feature interactions necessary for premise-hypothesis entailment, whereas vector quantization retains high-dimensional representational capacity. Enforcing a rigorous non-expansion constraint (|theta(M')| <= |theta(M_0)|), we identify 80 architectures that cause net parameter growth. All fine-tuned checkpoints are public on Hugging Face (https://huggingface.co/Hooshaai) and complete evaluation pipelines are open-sourced at https://github.com/Hooshaai/paper-a3.
