TITLE
-----
Toward Linear Attention: A Comprehensive Comparison of Over 200 Attention Mechanisms

ABSTRACT
--------
Transformer attention is quadratic in sequence length, motivating a large and rapidly growing family of alternative attention mechanisms — from low-rank and linear approximations to recurrent, sparse, codebook-based, and mixture-of-experts variants. Despite this proliferation, no systematic comparison of these mechanisms exists, and it remains unclear which families preserve reasoning capacity on multi-class tasks and which collapse under architectural constraints. We conduct a comprehensive study of over 200 attention mechanisms and compression methods on DistilBERT-base, evaluating each under a unified pipeline across three benchmarks: SST-2 (binary sentiment), MNLI (three-class natural language inference), and AG News (four-class topic classification). Our evaluation covers linear attention (Performer, Linformer, Cosformer, Nystromformer), recurrent and SSM-based layers (HGRN2, Mamba, Samba, Jamba, DeltaNet), mixture-of-experts attention (BAM-UMoE, Expert-Choice MoE), low-rank and SVD variants (a3, ASVD, Adaptive-SVD, Swift-SVD), vector quantization (k-means), and sparse attention (NSA, Hamming, Salt), among many others. We evaluate every mechanism as provided, without forcing any specific compression or linearization, and report accuracy together with its parameter cost — including mechanisms that slightly expand the parameter budget. Our analysis reveals two dominant regimes for multi-class reasoning: rank-truncation methods achieve 90.71% on SST-2 at 1.05× compression but collapse to 72.20% on MNLI, while vector quantization preserves the full coordinate span and achieves 81.40% on MNLI at 2.73× compression with 11.7× lower reconstruction error. These findings provide the first unified comparison of attention mechanisms across compression profiles, revealing which families preserve reasoning and which do not. All fine-tuned checkpoints and evaluation pipelines are publicly available.
