TITLE
-----
Rank Truncation vs. Vector Quantization: A Systematic Study of Attention Compression for Multi-Class Reasoning

ABSTRACT
--------
Attention compression reduces the memory footprint of transformer models, but the trade-offs between divergent mathematical regimes remain poorly understood. Existing studies evaluate only a handful of methods in isolation, making it difficult to determine which compression approaches preserve reasoning capacity on multi-class tasks. We conduct a systematic study of 277 attention variants and compression methods on DistilBERT-base, evaluating each across three benchmarks: SST-2 (binary sentiment), MNLI (three-class natural language inference), and AG News (four-class topic classification). Our central contribution is the identification of two distinct mathematical regimes: low-rank truncation and vector quantization. Evaluating every method under a strict non-expansion guarantee (|theta(M')| <= |theta(M_0)|), we find that rank-truncation methods (e.g., SVD-based a3) achieve 90.71% on SST-2 at 1.05x compression but drop sharply to 72.20% on MNLI (a 9.60-point drop from the 81.80% baseline). In contrast, vector quantization methods (e.g., k-means) preserve the full coordinate span and achieve 81.40% on MNLI at 2.73x compression with 11.7x lower reconstruction error (0.032 vs. 0.377), yielding a +9.20-point advantage over SVD on cross-sentence inference. Furthermore, our non-expansion constraint disqualifies 80 architectures that silently increase model parameters by up to 139.45%. These findings reveal that vector quantization is essential for preserving high-dimensional representations in cross-sentence reasoning, while rank truncation suffices only for single-sentence tasks. All fine-tuned checkpoints and evaluation pipelines are publicly available.
