\section{Limitations and Failure Modes}

\subsection{Calibration Dependency}
The efficacy of \texttt{AdaptiveRanks} depends on representative calibration sequences. Distributional shift between calibration tokens and deployment data can introduce representation degradation.

\subsection{Hardware and Architectural Constraints}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rank Granularity:} Factorized ranks should ideally align with tensor core dimensions or head dimension tiles ($r \pmod 8 == 0$) to avoid irregular tensor padding.
\item \textbf{Kernel Launch Overhead:} On short sequences ($N < 16$), executing dual factorized matrix products incurs launch overhead compared to a single fused GEMM.
\item \textbf{Rank Redundancy Ceiling:} When selected energy-thresholded rank $r \ge \frac{d_{\text{in}} d_{\text{out}}}{d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}}}$, low-rank factorization expands total parameter footprint. Our implementation enforces explicit parameter non-expansion guards to skip factorization on such layers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Numerical Precision Limits}
Aggressive low-rank truncation can cause floating-point underflow under 16-bit precision, requiring explicit safety clamping ($10^{-4}$) to prevent numerical divergence.

\subsection{Long-Context Activation Memory Footprints}
While low-rank factorization substantially curtails static parameter volume, it does not intrinsically alter the quadratic activation storage demanded by standard softmax self-attention over extended sequence contexts ($N > 4{,}096$). Consequently, for extreme long-context generation tasks, \texttt{AdaptiveRanks} must be paired with flash-attention kernels or state-space sequence modeling layers to mitigate activation out-of-memory errors.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Non-Uniform Calibration Sequences}
Calibration covariance matrices calculated over small token batches ($N_{\text{calib}} < 256$) display empirical sample variance that can perturb optimal singular projection subspaces. We recommend gathering diverse multi-domain calibration corpora to ensure singular subspace estimation remains representative of runtime distribution shifts.
