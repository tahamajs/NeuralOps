\section{Background}

\subsection{Softmax Attention}

For input $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N \times d}$:

\[
Q = X W\_Q, \quad K = X W\_K, \quad V = X W\_V
\tag{3.1}
\]

\[
\text{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d\_h}}\right) V
\tag{3.2}
\]

The softmax kernel $e^{q \cdot k}$ is positive-definite and gives the 
attention matrix a probabilistic interpretation.

\subsection{Why Softmax Is Hard to Kernelize}

For the softmax:

\[
\text{softmax}(s\_{ij}) = \frac{e^{s\_{ij}}}{\sum\_{j'} e^{s\_{ij'}}}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The exponential $e^{s_{ij}} = \exp(q_i \cdot k_j)$ has a positive-definite 
kernel, but the explicit feature map is \textbf{infinite-dimensional}:

\[
\exp(x \cdot y) = \sum\_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(x \cdot y)^n}{n!}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Truncating at order $d$ gives a finite feature map but with exponentially 
many features ($\binom{d + d_h}{d}$). This motivates \textbf{random} feature 
approximations (RFF, PORF).

\subsection{Polynomial Kernel}

A simple alternative is the \textbf{polynomial kernel}:

\[
K(q, k) = (1 + q \cdot k)^d
\tag{3.5}
\]

Expansion:

\[
(1 + q \cdot k)^d = \sum\_{n=0}^d \binom{d}{n} (q \cdot k)^n
\tag{3.6}
\]

\textbf{Feature map.} $\phi(x) = \left( \sqrt{\binom{d}{n}} x^{\otimes n} \right)_{n=0}^d$ with dimension 
$\binom{d + d_h}{d}$.

For small $d$ (e.g., $d = 2$), the feature map is tractable:
\begin{itemize}
\item $d = 1$: dimension $1 + d_h$
\item $d = 2$: dimension $1 + d_h + d_h^2$

\end{itemize}
\subsection{PADRe Kernel}

PADRe uses the normalized version:

\[
K\_\text{PADRe}(q, k) = \left(1 + \frac{s}{\text{order}}\right)^\text{order}
\tag{3.7}
\]

where $s = (q \cdot k)/\sqrt{d_h}$ (with centering).

\textbf{Why the $1/\text{order}$ scaling?} As $\text{order} \to \infty$:

\[
\lim\_{d \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{s}{d}\right)^d = e^s
\tag{3.8}
\]

So PADRe is a \textbf{Taylor approximation of softmax} with a specific 
scaling that keeps the base close to 1.

\subsection{Max-Centering for Stability}

Without centering, $s_{ij}$ can be large positive values, and 
$(1 + s/\text{order})^\text{order}$ overflows for large $s$.

\textbf{Solution.} Center over the max:

\[
s\_{ij} \leftarrow s\_{ij} - \max\_j s\_{ij}
\tag{3.9}
\]

This ensures $s_{ij} \leq 0$, so $1 + s_{ij}/\text{order} \leq 1$, and 
the result is bounded.

\subsection{LoRA Recovery}

LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) parameterizes weight updates:

\[
\Delta W = \frac{\alpha}{r\_L} B A
\tag{3.10}
\]

with $B = 0$ at initialization. Used for post-substitution recovery.

\subsection{Grouped-Query Attention (GQA)}

For $H_{kv} < H$:

\[
K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N \times H\_{kv} \times d\_h}
\tag{3.11}
\]

Expanded to $H$ heads via \texttt{repeat\_kv}.

\subsection{Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)}
